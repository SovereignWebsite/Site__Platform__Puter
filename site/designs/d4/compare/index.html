Puter, OpenRouter and NVIDIA as routes to models

A short comparison, facts only. It covers one question: can a static page reach a model with the visitor paying, and no key of ours in the page? Sister sites go deeper: the OpenRouter guide is live. An NVIDIA guide is planned and has no address yet.

Three routes, as read or tested on 25 November 2026
QuestionPuter.jsOpenRouterNVIDIA API catalogue
How the visitor connectsSigns in to their Puter account in a pop-up (Puter docs: auth.signIn(), read 25 November 2026).OAuth with PKCE, using a code challenge with method S256 (OpenRouter's OAuth PKCE guide, read 25 November 2026).Not tested by us. A key is needed.
Who paysThe visitor, from their Puter allowance (Puter docs: User-Pays Model, read 25 November 2026).The visitor's own OpenRouter account.Not applicable to a visitor route.
Key in the pageNone (Puter docs: User-Pays Model, read 25 November 2026).The OAuth flow returns a key to the browser. Where the page keeps it is the page's choice.A key is needed, so it would sit in the page or behind a proxy.
Browser calls to the APIHandled by the Puter.js script.Tested by us: a cross-origin preflight to openrouter.ai/api/v1/chat/completions answered access-control-allow-origin: *.Tested by us: a preflight to integrate.api.nvidia.com/v1/chat/completions returned no access-control-* headers, so a browser would block the call.
Privacy statement on AIPolicy does not mention AI, as above (Puter's privacy policy, read 25 November 2026).Not assessed here.Describes the catalogue as a place to "build POCs with model APIs" before moving to self-hosting (NVIDIA's NIM FAQ, read 25 November 2026).

The tests marked "tested by us" were single requests on 25 November 2026 and may change. Re-run them before you rely on them.

What this does not say: which route is best. That depends on what the page holds and who it is for.
